<ul>
  <li><strong>Standard density:</strong> Good all-around bowls that bowlers buy when they want to finish the season with the same bowls they started with. Work well across varying conditions.</li>
  <li><strong>High-density:</strong> Smaller in the hand for a given weight. Bowls pull up faster on a quick green rather than sliding past the jack, but require more effort on slow turf.</li>
  <li><strong>Low-density:</strong> Larger for their weight. Similar in size to traditional wooden bowls, running easier across heavy or damp greens.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Weight</strong></h3>

<p>When choosing a bowl, weight is an important factor to consider. Bowls range in weight from 2lb to 3lb (typically 2lb 4oz to 2lb 12oz).</p>

<p>Generally, heavier bowls will run truer over distances and be less easily knocked away by opponents' bowls. For men, 2lb 8oz to 2lb 10oz is standard; for ladies, 2lb 4oz to 2lb 8oz is most common.</p>

<h2><strong>The Best Crown Green Bowls for Beginners</strong></h2>

<p>Here are our detailed recommendations for top beginner crown green models:</p>

<h3><strong>Drakes Pride Professional Crown Green Bowls</strong></h3>

<p>Drakes Pride bowls have a wider and flatter sole than Taylor's bowls, providing superior traction on slope-heavy crowns. They feel very comfortable in the hand because they are slightly narrower between the mounts.</p>

<h3><strong>Thomas Taylor Crown Green Bowls</strong></h3>

<p>Taylor's crown green bowls feature a taller, thinner sole profile, allowing them to peg higher across contours while holding a smooth, gradual finish. Models with the 205/6 or Deluxe grip offer exceptional hand purchase in wet grass.</p>

<h3><strong>Henselite Crown Green Bowls</strong></h3>

<p>The Henselite 21st Century crown green bowls offer proven quality with Henselite's popular Super Grip at a competitive price point, providing reliable curve repeatability across undulating greens.</p>

<p>Many bowlers appreciate the grip, similar to that seen on level green bowls. Please remember that this product line does not include mounts; this does not affect performance, but some bowlers prefer them.</p>

<h2><strong>What is The Price of A Lawn Bowl Set?</strong></h2>

<p>This may well be the deciding factor for many people after determining their ideal weight and density. The more unique a design style you choose, the higher the price tag.</p>

<p>Because new bowls can cost up to £350, it is recommended that you buy a set of used bowls for your first set. Secondhand bowls typically cost between £30 and £120, making them cost-effective.</p>

<p>Having the right and appropriate equipment is beneficial, but it is up to the player to put it to good use! A high-density bowl may be the best option for experienced players looking for an edge. </p>
