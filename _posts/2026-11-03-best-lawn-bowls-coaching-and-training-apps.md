<h2>1. Torny — Best for AI-Powered Shot Tracking &amp; Season Analytics</h2>

<p><strong>Torny</strong> is built specifically for bowlers who want to stop relying on gut feel and start tracking where their woods actually finish. Instead of just remembering the two great touchers you drew in a roll-up, Torny lets you record shot-by-shot outcomes across guided or freestyle practice sessions.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>How it helps your game:</strong> Its AI performance engine crunches your session logs to highlight patterns you might miss—such as consistently dropping short on backhand draws over 27 metres or struggling when transitioning from slow to fast rinks.</li>
  <li><strong>Who it suits best:</strong> Competitive club bowlers and county aspirants who want a digital logbook across an entire indoor or outdoor campaign.</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. Lawn Bowls Academy — Best for Gamified Mobile Drill Libraries</h2>

<p>If you like having your practice drills displayed step-by-step on your phone screen at the mat, the <strong>Lawn Bowls Academy</strong> app offers a structured library of routines covering draw weight, line control, and weighted positional shots.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>How it helps your game:</strong> When you first open the app, it profiles your experience level and suggests progressive challenges with built-in scoring so you have a clear benchmark to beat next week.</li>
  <li><strong>Prefer paper on the green?</strong> Many bowlers dislike unlocking their phone after every end in bright sunlight. If you prefer weather-proof printed sheets in your pocket, pair our <a href="{{ '/lawn-bowls-downloadable-drill-pack' | relative_url }}">Downloadable Lawn Bowls Drill Pack</a> with a quick post-session summary in your notes app.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. BowlsIQ — Best for Rink Mapping &amp; Tactical Head Analysis</h2>

<p><strong>BowlsIQ</strong> approaches improvement from a data and tactical angle. Rather than focusing purely on biomechanics, it teaches bowlers how to map the head, understand shot dispersion zones (the "cone of error" around your aiming line), and call higher-percentage shots.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>How it helps your game:</strong> It reinforces a core principle we teach in our <a href="{{ '/help/line-and-length-in-lawn-bowls-how-to-win-at-bowls' | relative_url }}">Line and Length Guide</a> and <a href="{{ '/guide/using-aiming-points-in-lawn-bowls' | relative_url }}">Aiming Points Guide</a>: finishing 18 inches behind the jack is twice as valuable as finishing 12 inches short.</li>
</ul>

<h2>4. WGTS (Who's Got The Shot?) — Best for Augmented-Reality Measuring</h2>

<p>We have all had friendly roll-ups or club bounce games where three bowls sit roughly two feet from the jack and nobody wants to bend down with a string measure four times. <strong>WGTS (Who's Got The Shot?)</strong> uses your smartphone's camera sensors and augmented reality (AR) to measure edge-to-edge distances in centimetres from above the head.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Important caveat for competitive play:</strong> While AR measuring apps are fantastic for practice drills and casual roll-ups, official club and league matches still require physical measures, callipers, and wedges. Read our guide on <a href="{{ '/help/lawn-bowls-how-to-measure' | relative_url }}">How to Measure in Lawn Bowls</a> for official World Bowls procedures.</li>
</ul>

<h2>5. CoachNow (and Smartphone Slow-Motion) — Best for Delivery Video Analysis</h2>

<p>Because a lawn bowls delivery happens in less than two seconds, spotting a subtle fault—like your hips opening up on the backhand or releasing the bowl an inch too high—is nearly impossible without video. <strong>CoachNow</strong> (along with your phone's built-in 120fps slow-motion camera) lets you scrub frame-by-frame and draw alignment lines over your anchor foot and shoulder plane.</p>

</ul>
