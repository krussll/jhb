<h2><a href="#how-to-choose-a-set-of-bowls"></a>How to choose a set of bowls</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There are multiple things to consider when selecting a set of bowls as a lead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Firstly, what is the bias characteristic of the model? As a lead it is generally considered better to buy bowls with a narrow line. The reasoning being that less movement on the swing will lead to a smaller difference between shots. Also, as a lead there is unlikely to be many other bowls blocking your chosen hand, this leaves you free to find the model with the best line for you. Whilst a narrow bowl isn’t a guarantee for consistency it is usually a safe bet.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A strong draw shot required for lead is built on consistency. This is also true for the bowls you use. All major brands use computer controlled machines to manufacture their woods, which makes variation between individual bowls undetectable. For this reason we recommend new, or near new bowls.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The final part of a bowl’s line characteristics is it’s “finish”. This describes how bowl acts as it slows down and finishes its movement towards the jack (hopefully!). A lead will require a bowl that has a “flat” finish, that is to say it follows a consistent path from its normal arc of travel. A model with a hooking finish can add extra movement, which in turn adds extra variability, which we are looking to remove.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Finally, where are you planning on playing? Some models focus either on indoor, or outdoor play whereas others try to be multi-purpose. Make sure you get the most on your purchase, especially if you play year round.</p>

<h2>Detailed Reviews of the Top 4 Bowls for Leads</h2>

<h3>1. Taylor Vector VS &mdash; <span class="badge badge-success">#1 Pick for Leads</span></h3>

<p>With its exceptionally clean, narrow running line and steady holding finish, the <strong>Taylor Vector VS</strong> is widely considered the ultimate lead bowl in the modern game. Originally developed for fast Australian greens and indoor play, it has become a staple for UK front-end players looking to eliminate wide, sweeping swings.</p>

<p>When bowling as lead, your objective is to draw directly to the jack without fighting unpredictable late movement. The Vector VS delivers precisely that: a lenient trajectory with almost zero end-of-rink kick. When tested on 14-to-16 second turf, it holds its line under crosswinds and stays firmly on the mat.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Bias Profile:</strong> Narrow bias (Rating: 3 / 10).</li>
  <li><strong>Finish Characteristic:</strong> Flat holding finish &mdash; holds true into the head.</li>
  <li><strong>Optimal Playing Speed:</strong> 14+ seconds grass, artificial rinks, and indoor carpet.</li>
  <li><strong>Grip Options:</strong> Taylor Progrip and Half-Pipe grip.</li>

<h3>2. Drakes Pride D-Tec &mdash; <span class="badge badge-primary">Indoor &amp; Fast Surface Specialist</span></h3>

<p>The <strong>D-Tec</strong> is Drakes Pride's flagship narrow-biased model. Engineered specifically for indoor needlefelt rinks and rapid outdoor surfaces, it has one of the straightest drawing profiles of any UK-manufactured bowl.</p>

<p>For leads who struggle with taking too much green on quick carpets, the D-Tec is a revelation. You can aim much tighter to the jack, reducing the margin of error between shots. However, keep in mind that on slow, heavy grass (under 12 seconds), its tight line can struggle to pull in, so we primarily recommend the D-Tec for summer turf and indoor bowlers.</p>

<ul>

<p>Not every lead has the luxury of playing on fast, billiard-table surfaces. In the early months of the UK season, wet and heavy greens can cause ultra-narrow bowls to stop short of the head. For these conditions, the <strong>Drakes Pride Professional</strong> is the ultimate reliable companion.</p>

<p>Its mid-narrow bias allows you to deliver with confidence, knowing the bowl will curve smoothly around early-season rough patches. It has a slightly more pronounced hook than the Vector VS or D-Tec, but on holding grass, this finish helps the bowl settle gracefully beside the jack.</p>

<h2>3 Golden Rules for Lead Bowlers</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Prioritise Weight over Line:</strong> A bowl that is the right length but 18 inches wide is still a counting shot; a bowl on line that is 4 yards short is useless. Build a repeatable arm swing.</li>
  <li><strong>Stick to Your Stronger Hand:</strong> When the head is open on the first end, deliver on whichever hand gives you the most confidence in reading green speed.</li>
  <li><strong>Keep the Running Surface Clean:</strong> Keep a microfibre towel or grip cloth handy to wipe dew and grass clippings from the running track before stepping onto the mat.</li>
</ol>
