faqs:
  - question: "Can I try lawn bowls at a club without buying my own bowls?"
    answer: "Yes, almost every bowling club maintains sets of club loan bowls in various sizes and weights that visitors and prospective members can borrow free of charge for open days, coaching sessions, or casual roll-ups. Club coaches and experienced bowlers will measure your hand and help you pick a comfortable size before you step onto the green."
  - question: "How much does it cost to join a local bowls club?"
    answer: "Annual full bowling membership typically ranges between £60 and £180 in the UK (or $150 to $350 AUD in Australia), with many clubs offering discounted introductory rates (often 50% off) for first-year novices. Casual barefoot bowls or green fees for non-members generally cost between £3 and £10 (or $10 to $20 AUD) per session including bowl and shoe hire."
  - question: "What is the difference between flat green, crown green, and indoor bowls clubs?"
    answer: "Flat green bowls is played on a level grass or synthetic surface in defined rectangular rinks outdoors during summer (April–September). Crown green bowls is played on an undulating, curved grass green where bowls can be delivered in any direction. Indoor bowls takes place on fast, level needle-punch carpet rinks inside purpose-built stadiums, running throughout autumn and winter (September–April)."
  - question: "What shoes do I need for my first club roll-up?"
    answer: "To protect the bowling surface, clubs require smooth, completely flat-soled footwear with no heels, ridges, or deep treads. For your first taster session or barefoot bowls session, flat sneakers (such as Vans or Converse) or playing in socks/bare feet (where permitted) is usually fine. If you join as a regular bowler, you will want dedicated regulation flat-soled bowling shoes."
  - question: "Do I need to wear formal bowling whites on my first visit?"
    answer: "No. Traditional bowling whites or club uniform colours are only required during inter-club league matches, weekend tournaments, or formal club championships. For open days, beginner coaching sessions, and casual roll-ups, comfortable casual sportswear (such as polo shirts, neat shorts, chinos, or tracksuit bottoms) is universally accepted across modern clubs."

Whether you are looking to take up lawn bowls for the first time, searching for a competitive club to play weekend pennants, or tracking down an indoor stadium to keep your game sharp through the winter months, finding the right local club is your first step onto the green.

Lawn bowls is one of the most welcoming, social, and accessible sports in the world. Clubs across the UK, Australia, New Zealand, North America, and beyond operate throughout the calendar year—from traditional outdoor grass greens in summer to heated all-weather indoor carpet facilities in winter.

<h2>How to Choose and Visit Your First Bowls Club</h2>

<p>Visiting a bowls club for the first time is straightforward and casual, but knowing what to expect removes any hesitation. Modern clubs are community-focused hubs eager to introduce newcomers of all ages to the game.</p>

<h3>1. Understand the Different Bowling Formats</h3>

<p>Before choosing a venue, consider which style of bowls suits your schedule and seasonal preferences:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Outdoor Flat Green Bowls:</strong> The classic international format, played on square, level greens divided into parallel rinks. In the UK, Ireland, and Canada, the outdoor season runs from mid-April through late September. In Australia, South Africa, and warm climates, outdoor play continues year-round.</li>
  <li><strong>Indoor Stadium Bowls:</strong> Played on purpose-built needle-punch carpet rinks in climate-controlled indoor arenas. The primary indoor season runs from September through April, providing a weather-proof, warm winter haven for bowlers. Indoor rinks are exceptionally fast, true, and consistent.</li>
  <li><strong>Crown Green Bowls:</strong> Popular throughout Northern England, the West Midlands, and North Wales. The green features a raised center (crown) with natural slopes, and bowls can be delivered in any direction across the turf.</li>
  <li><strong>Short Mat Bowls:</strong> Played on portable 40–45ft carpets rolled out in village halls, community centres, and sports clubs year-round. Learn how it works in our <a href="{{ '/guide/guide-to-short-mat-bowls/' | relative_url }}">complete guide to short mat bowls</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Social &amp; Barefoot Bowls:</strong> A relaxed, fun atmosphere with music, food, and drinks, where strict dress codes are replaced by flat sneakers or bare feet. Read our <a href="{{ '/help/barefoot-bowls/' | relative_url }}">guide to barefoot bowls</a> for rules and etiquette.</li>
</ul>

<h3>2. First-Timer's Club Checklist</h3>

<p>Follow this step-by-step checklist when planning your first visit to a local bowling club:</p>

<div class="club-checklist">
  <div class="club-checklist__card">
    <div class="club-checklist__card-header">
      <h4 class="club-checklist__card-title">1. Attend an Open Day or Free Taster</h4>
      <span class="club-badge club-badge--gold">Step 1</span>
    </div>
    <p class="club-checklist__card-desc">
      Most clubs host spring open days (such as Bowls England's <em>Big Bowls Weekend</em>) with free equipment, friendly coaching, and a cup of tea or clubhouse drink. Contact the club secretary or check their website beforehand.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="club-checklist__card">
    <div class="club-checklist__card-header">
      <h4 class="club-checklist__card-title">2. Borrow Club Loan Bowls</h4>
      <span class="club-badge club-badge--gold">Step 2</span>
    </div>
    <p class="club-checklist__card-desc">
      Do not buy a set of bowls before visiting. Clubs keep cabinets of loan bowls in sizes 00 to 5. An experienced club member will measure your grip and hand size so you roll with the right fit from day one.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="club-checklist__card">
    <div class="club-checklist__card-header">
      <h4 class="club-checklist__card-title">3. Wear Smooth, Flat-Soled Shoes</h4>
      <span class="club-badge club-badge--gold">Step 3</span>
    </div>
    <p class="club-checklist__card-desc">
      Greens are delicate turf or precision woven carpets. Wear sneakers or casual shoes with completely flat, tread-free soles (e.g., Vans, Converse, or skate shoes). Many clubs permit socks or bare feet for beginners.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="club-checklist__card">
    <div class="club-checklist__card-header">
      <h4 class="club-checklist__card-title">4. Ask About Novice Membership Rates</h4>
      <span class="club-badge club-badge--gold">Step 4</span>
    </div>
    <p class="club-checklist__card-desc">
      Most clubs offer 50% discount subscriptions for first-year novices, alongside social non-playing memberships. This allows you to practice on roll-up afternoons and enjoy the clubhouse bar without heavy upfront costs.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<h3>3. Equipment &amp; Footwear Rules Explained</h3>

<p>When you start playing regular club roll-ups or enter club competitions, having appropriate gear ensures compliance with local association rules and boosts your confidence:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Choosing Your First Set of Bowls:</strong> Beginners should look for versatile, forgiving mid-bias models such as the Taylor Ace, Drakes Pride Professional, or Henselite Tiger II. If playing primarily on fast indoor carpets, check our guide to the <a href="{{ '/guide/best-lawn-bowls-for-indoors/' | relative_url }}">best lawn bowls for indoors</a>. For fast Australian greens running 15+ seconds, see our <a href="{{ '/guide/best-lawn-bowls-for-fast-greens/' | relative_url }}">best bowls for fast greens</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Regulation Footwear:</strong> While casual flat sneakers work for open days, clubs require regulation flat-soled bowling shoes for league fixtures. Read our review of the <a href="{{ '/guide/best-lawn-bowls-shoes/' | relative_url }}">best lawn bowls shoes</a> for comfortable, supportive options.</li>
  <li><strong>Club Dress Code:</strong> Casual roll-ups and coaching sessions generally welcome clean sportswear (polo shirts, tailored shorts, chinos). Whites or club colours are typically reserved for competitive matches. Learn the nuances in our full <a href="{{ '/help/lawn-bowls-dress-code/' | relative_url }}">lawn bowls dress code guide</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h3>4. Club Membership Fees &amp; Casual Playing Costs</h3>

<p>Lawn bowls remains one of the most affordable sports to join:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Annual Full Membership:</strong> Typically £60 to £180 in the UK or $150 to $350 AUD in Australia. This grants unlimited outdoor green access for roll-ups throughout the summer season.</li>
  <li><strong>First-Year Novice Discounts:</strong> Many clubs discount introductory memberships by 30% to 50% to encourage newcomers.</li>
  <li><strong>Indoor Green Fees (Rink Fees):</strong> Indoor stadium play typically incurs a nominal rink fee of £3.50 to £5.00 ($8 to $12 AUD) for a two-hour booked session.</li>
  <li><strong>Social / Non-Playing Membership:</strong> If you want to use the clubhouse lounge, bar, and attend social events without entering competitions, social memberships often cost just £15 to £30 ($25 to $50 AUD) per year.</li>
</ul>
